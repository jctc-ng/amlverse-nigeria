## One-sentence positioning

AMLVERSE Nigeria is a scenario-based learning town that helps financial intelligence and law-enforcement learners practise evidence-led judgement across AML/CFT/CPF, FIU cooperation, risk assessment, virtual assets and defensible intelligence reporting.

## Conference abstract (approx. 185 words)

Traditional training often asks practitioners to remember concepts, while operational work requires them to interpret incomplete evidence, understand lawful authority and communicate uncertainty. **AMLVERSE Nigeria** explores an accessible alternative: a guided, mobile-friendly learning environment that turns financial intelligence principles into synthetic investigations and sequential decisions.

The platform contains nine learning worlds, a full forty-Recommendation FATF curriculum, structured lessons, interactive evidence challenges, multi-stage decision dossiers, virtual-asset graph exercises, and an integrated capstone. Learners work through realistic but fictional records while distinguishing facts from hypotheses, FIU intelligence from evidence, risk indicators from guilt, and cross-border cooperation from unrestricted information disclosure.

A connected Learning Town enables guided progression, recorded milestones and optional free exploration. The platform uses no live investigation data and does not issue accredited qualifications.

The presentation would demonstrate the design and seek external feedback on technical accuracy, usability, accessibility, instructional value and jurisdictional adaptation. The next research stage is an independently reviewed pilot with defined pre/post learning outcomes and privacy safeguards. It would not claim improved practitioner performance without measured evidence.

## Suggested talk title

**From Standards to Investigative Judgement: Building an Interactive AML/CFT Learning Town for Financial Intelligence Practitioners**

## Live demonstration sequence (15 minutes)

1. **The problem (2 minutes):** memorising a rule versus using it in a case.
2. **Learning Town (3 minutes):** guided districts, locks, individual learning progress and optional unlocks.
3. **Evidence judgement (4 minutes):** one fictional FIU cooperation or financial transaction scenario; identify the attribution gap.
4. **Graph investigation (3 minutes):** follow synthetic wallets, avoid double-counting, and preserve uncertainty.
5. **Professional safeguards (2 minutes):** source dates, consent and confidentiality, synthetic data, standards versus national law.
6. **Invitation (1 minute):** request cross-jurisdiction subject-matter review and evaluation partnerships through public GitHub issues.

## Proposed research and international reach milestones

### First 30 days: credibility and review infrastructure

- Public creator attribution, transparent methodology and a complete institutional-independence statement.
- Mapping of learning objectives to authoritative public standards.
- A published editorial review process with correction reporting and change tracking.
- Keyboard/mobile/browser regression testing and an independent accessibility audit request.

### Days 31–60: external validation and controlled pilots

- Invite a small number of independent law-enforcement educators and AML/CFT subject-matter experts to review nominated modules.
- Obtain approval for any organisational pilot; use synthetic cases only.
- Compare pre/post learner understanding on evidence confidence, lawful information exchange and report writing.
- Record feedback and limitations as evidence, not marketing claims.

### Days 61–90: dissemination based on verified results

- Publish a transparent pilot note with sample size, methodology, outcomes and limitations.
- Prepare a conference demonstration and technical note for international FIU and law-enforcement education audiences.
- Explore jurisdiction-specific adaptations only with appropriate legal and subject-matter review.
- Consider multi-language accessibility after verifying English source content and maintaining translation review.
