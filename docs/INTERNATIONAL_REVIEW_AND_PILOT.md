# AMLVERSE Nigeria — International review and pilot framework

**Project creator and lead:** Muzammil Adamu

**Status:** Independently developed educational prototype. Not an official publication, accredited course, or endorsed programme of any financial intelligence unit, FATF, Egmont Group or UNODC. Neither independent peer review nor measured learning effectiveness is claimed at this stage.

## Purpose

AMLVERSE Nigeria uses fictional investigations and decisions to teach AML/CFT/CPF concepts, FIU cooperation, evidence attribution, virtual-asset analysis and written financial intelligence. The Nigeria edition draws on international principles while recognising that investigators, supervisors, FIUs and prosecutors have different powers and jurisdiction-specific obligations.

## Editorial safeguards

1. **Authoritative source first.** Each standards-based lesson should cite the current primary publication, amendment date, relevant sections and interpretation notes.
2. **Qualified legal treatment.** Explicitly distinguish international recommendations from binding national law and transition deadlines.
3. **Synthetic scenarios only.** No actual STRs, user account records, real operational intelligence or victim identifiers in repositories, screenshots, feedback or public issue tickets.
4. **Observation versus inference.** Transaction records, chain events and documents must be separated from attribution hypotheses, risk indicators and legal conclusions.
5. **Review logs.** Capture the content issue, primary source, reviewer discipline, proposed correction, reviewer decision and resolution date in public issues without personal data.
6. **No false endorsement.** Referencing FATF, Egmont or national agencies does not signal a partnership or approval. Do not display official logos without permission.
7. **Accessibility and quality.** Test keyboard controls, touch targets, clear navigation, screen-reader text and mobile layouts.
8. **Change control.** Recheck content after major revisions of FATF Standards and applicable public legal frameworks.

## Proposed evaluation protocol (not yet executed)

**Research question:** Does scenario-based learning improve a defined set of AML/CFT/CPF reasoning skills compared with pre-session knowledge?

- Invite voluntary participants through approved institutions or educators, with transparent informed consent and local ethics / organisational authorisation when needed.
- Specify learning outcomes before recruitment: risk identification, evidential qualification, lawful cooperation, source assessment and written inference.
- Design a comparable baseline and post-learning assessment, preferably reviewed by independent practitioners; do not reuse the exact answer sequence.
- Collect strictly minimal learner feedback; use aggregate findings and avoid real case records.
- Predefine measures such as correct evidence classification, reasons for decisions, confidence calibration, completion, and usability by device.
- Report denominators, missing data, learning gains, uncertainty and limitations without claiming causality beyond the design.
- Invite external expert review before public presentation or claims of effectiveness.
